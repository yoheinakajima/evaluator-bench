# Related work, annotated

Grouped by what each piece contributes to the two datasets. Retrieved 14 Sep 2026 unless noted.

## Frameworks for third-party AI evaluation and audit

- Brundage et al. 2020, *Toward Trustworthy AI Development: Mechanisms for Supporting Verifiable Claims* (arXiv 2004.07213). Early call for third-party auditing, red teaming, bias bounties. Milestone 2020 in Dataset B.
- Raji, Xu, Honigsberg, Ho 2022, *Outsider Oversight* (AIES). Audit ecosystem design with explicit analogies to financial audit and rating agencies, including mandatory rotation. Closest prior in spirit; no measurement.
- Anderljung et al. 2023, *Towards Publicly Accountable Frontier LLMs* (arXiv 2311.14711). ASPIRE: access, searching attitude, proportionality, independence, resources, expertise. Source of two rubric dimensions.
- Casper et al. 2024, *Black-Box Access is Insufficient for Rigorous AI Audits*. Access levels; feeds the access dimension.
- Homewood et al. 2025, *Third-party compliance reviews for frontier AI safety frameworks* (arXiv 2505.01643). Compliance review design.
- Schuett 2025, *Frontier AI developers need an internal audit function* (Risk Analysis). Internal audit; useful for the first-party contrast.
- Manheim et al. 2025, *The necessity of AI audit standards boards* (AI & Society). Standards board proposal with aviation, nuclear, accounting, pharma precedents.
- Brundage et al. 2026, *Frontier AI Auditing* (arXiv 2601.11699). AI Assurance Levels 1 to 4; precedents cited include FAA history, UL, melamine, HackerOne. The framework the paper should position against.
- Charnock et al. 2026, *Expanding External Access to Frontier AI Models for Dangerous Capability Evaluations* (arXiv 2601.11916). Access taxonomy mapped to the EU Code.
- AI Evaluator Forum 2025, AEF-1. Five operating conditions: sufficient access and resources, minimized conflicts, analytic autonomy, transparent methods and results, protection of sensitive information.
- International AI Safety Report 2026 (arXiv 2602.21012), sections on third-party evaluation.

## Empirical studies of auditors and audits

- Costanza-Chock, Raji, Buolamwini 2022, *Who Audits the Auditors?* (FAccT). Field scan and survey of algorithmic auditors. The paper's empirical ancestor; different population and method.
- Groves et al. 2025, *Auditing the Audits: Lessons for Algorithmic Accountability from Local Law 144's Bias Audits* (FAccT). Coded 116 published bias audits; shows what an audit-of-audits looks like.
- Costanza-Chock et al. and Raji and Buolamwini 2019, *Actionable Auditing*. Effect of public naming.
- SaferAI ratings of frontier safety frameworks; FLI AI Safety Index. Score labs, not evaluators; useful as the complementary axis.

## Independence in other assurance regimes

- Financial audit: Healy and Palepu 2003, *The Fall of Enron* (J. Econ. Perspectives); DeFond and Zhang 2014, *A review of archival auditing research* (J. Accounting and Economics), for evidence on rotation, fees, and independence.
- Credit ratings: Griffin and Tang 2011, *Did credit rating agencies make unbiased assumptions on CDOs?* (AER); White 2010, *Markets: The credit rating agencies* (J. Econ. Perspectives), on the issuer-pays shift and NRSRO regulation.
- Product safety: UL, *Engineering Progress* (2016); OSHA NRTL program history.
- Drug testing: the Industrial Bio-Test Laboratories case and the 1978 GLP regulations; Carpenter 2010, *Reputation and Power* (Princeton), on FDA.
- Aviation: the 2020 Aircraft Certification, Safety, and Accountability Act and the House Transportation Committee's 737 MAX report (2020) on delegation.
- Nuclear: Rees 1994, *Hostages of Each Other* (Chicago), on INPO as industry self-regulation with insurance and NRC backstop.
- Ship classification: Boisson 1994, *Classification societies and safety at sea*; EU Regulation 391/2009.
- Cybersecurity: Common Criteria criticisms; Anderson and Moore 2006, *The Economics of Information Security* (Science).
- Regulatory markets: Hadfield and Clark 2023, *Regulatory Markets: The Future of AI Governance*, for the licensed-private-regulator model the AI field keeps circling.
